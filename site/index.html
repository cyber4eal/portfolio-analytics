Advice

Mechanical consequences of the numbers, not recommendations. Four theories are run side by side because they optimise different things — where they agree you can be confident, and where they diverge is where the assumptions are doing the work.

What compounds fastest

Money compounds, so what gets maximised is expected log return: growth ≈ return − volatility²∕2. That second term is why a lower-returning mix can leave you richer, and why past a point more risk makes you poorer.

Hedges that hold up when it matters

Ranked by what each does to compounding at its own best size — not by how much volatility it removes, because a hedge that costs more return than the variance it saves makes you poorer slowly. The last two columns are the honest test: correlation on ordinary days against correlation on your worst 10%.

How to get there

The trades that move today's book toward the growth-optimal mix, cut to what one month can actually do given your cash and free sells. Trend does not change the destination — it only decides which trades go first.

Risk surfaces

Two shapes a flat chart cannot show. The blend surface sweeps every mix of two funds so you can see the whole trade-off at once instead of reading it off eighteen table rows. The correlation surface plots each holding's correlation to the rest of the book through time — because diversification is not a number, it is a number that moves, and it moves most at exactly the moment you were relying on it. Drag to rotate, scroll to zoom.
